# Application of Integrals 
*Created on:* 2024-12-23  
*Tags:* #LineIntegral #SurfaceIntegral #VolumeIntegral

## Line Integral

If $f$ is continuously differentiable on the closed interval $[a,b]$, the length of the curve $y=f(x)$ from $x=a$ to $x=b$ is
$$
L = \int_{b}^a \sqrt{1 + \left( \frac{dy}{dx} \right)^2 }\,dx = \int_{b}^a \sqrt{1 + \left( f\,'(x) \right)^2 }\,dx 
$$
## Surface Integrals

### Revolution about the x-Axis

The area of the surface generated by revolving the curve $y=f(x)$ about the $x$ axis is 
$$
S = \int_{b}^a 2\pi y \sqrt{ 1+\left( \frac{dy}{dx} \right)^2 }\,dx = \int_{b}^a 2\pi f(x) \sqrt{ 1+f'(x)^2 }\,dx
$$
### Revolution about the y-Axis

The area of the surface generated by revolving the curve $x = g(y)$ about the $y$ axis is 
$$
S = \int_{b}^a 2\pi x \sqrt{ 1+\left( \frac{dx}{dy} \right)^2 }\,dy = \int_{b}^a 2\pi g(y) \sqrt{ 1+\left(g'(y) \right)^2 }\,dy
$$
## Volume Integral

The volume of a solid of known integrable cross-section area $A(x)$ from $x=a$ to $x=b$ is the integral of $A$ from $a$ to $b$,
$$
V = \int_{b}^a A(x)\,dx
$$

### Solid of Revolution: The Disk Method

$$
V = \int_{b}^a A(x)\,dx = \int_{b}^a \pi [R(x)]^2\,dx
$$
### Solid of Revolution: The Washer Method

$$
V = \int_{b}^a A(x)\,dx = \int_{b}^a \pi [R(x)^2 - r(x)^2]\,dx
$$

### Shell Formula for Revolution about a Vertical Line

$$
V = \int_{a}^b 2\pi (\text{Shell Height}) (\text{Shell Radius})\,dx
$$
