# Directional Lateral Stability 
*Created on:* 2025-01-29

## Directional Stability

- Stability in the yaw direction is called directional stability.
- Vertical stabilizer is the conventional mechanism for directional stability.
- The vertical tail is designed with a symmetric airfoil section, is at a zero angle of attack to the free stream, and it experiences no net aerodynamic force perpendicular to $V_{\infty}$.
- At a disturbed position, the tail experiences an aerodynamic force perpendicular to $V_{\infty}$. This force creates a restoring yawing moment about the center of gravity.
- The magnitude of the restoring moment in yaw is equal to $F_{vt}l_{vt}$ where $l_{vt}$ is the moment arm from the aerodynamic center of the vertical tail to the airplane's center of gravity.
- The vertical tail volume ratio governs the directional stability of the aircraft.
$$
V_{vt} = \frac{l_{vt}S_{vt}}{bS}
$$


## Lateral Stability

- Stability in roll is called lateral stability.
- Lift equals the weight and they act equal and opposite to each other, there is no net side force.
- Due to disturbance, the right wing dips and a roll to the right ensues.
- The lift angle is also rotated from the vertical through angle $\phi$, which results in a side force and cause sideslip.
- If the aircraft wings are in the same plane, equal lift is generated by them and no restoring moment is generated.
- In case of a dihedral, the angle on the right wing is $\theta_{1}=\theta-\Gamma$ and the angle on the right wing is $\theta_{2}=\theta+\Gamma$. This difference on angle causes unequal lift and hence creates a restoring rolling moment.
