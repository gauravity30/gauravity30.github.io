# Classification of Aircrafts 
*Created on:* 2025-01-20  
*Tags:* #Classification 
*Course:* [[Flight Mechanics]]

---
## Based on Propulsion
- **Powered Aircraft**: These have engines to generate thrust.
    - **Propeller-driven Aircraft**: Use propellers for thrust (e.g., Cessna 172).
    - **Jet Aircraft**: Use jet engines for propulsion (e.g., Boeing 747, F-16).
    - **Turboprop Aircraft**: Combine jet and propeller propulsion (e.g., ATR 72).
    - **Rocket-propelled Aircraft**: Use rockets for propulsion (e.g., Space Shuttle).
- **Unpowered Aircraft**: Do not have an engine and rely on external forces.
    - **Gliders**: Use air currents to stay aloft.
    - **Kites**: Tethered and depend on wind.
## Based on Wing Configuration
- **Fixed-wing Aircraft**: Have stationary wings relative to the fuselage.
    - **Conventional Wings**: Horizontal wings for lift.
    - **Delta Wings**: Triangular shape (e.g., Concorde).
    - **Swept Wings**: Angled back for high-speed efficiency.
    - **Variable-sweep Wings**: Adjustable wing sweep (e.g., F-14 Tomcat).
- **Rotary-wing Aircraft**: Use rotating blades to generate lift.
    - **Helicopters**: Vertical takeoff and landing (VTOL).
    - **Gyroplanes**: Rotor spins freely, propulsion from an engine.
- **Hybrid Wing Types**: Combine features (e.g., tiltrotor aircraft like V-22 Osprey).
## Based on Purpose
- **Commercial Aircraft**: For passenger and cargo transport.
    - **Passenger Jets**: Transport people (e.g., Airbus A320).
    - **Cargo Aircraft**: Transport goods (e.g., Boeing 747 Freighter).
- **Military Aircraft**: Used by armed forces.
    - **Fighters**: Air superiority (e.g., F-22 Raptor).
    - **Bombers**: Strategic strikes (e.g., B-2 Spirit).
    - **Transport**: Troop/cargo movement (e.g., C-130 Hercules).
    - **Reconnaissance**: Surveillance (e.g., U-2 Spy Plane).
- **General Aviation Aircraft**: Private, business, or recreational use.
    - **Small Airplanes**: For personal travel (e.g., Piper PA-28).
    - **Corporate Jets**: Business use (e.g., Gulfstream G650).
- **Specialized Aircraft**: Built for specific tasks.
    - **Agricultural Aircraft**: Crop dusting.
    - **Firefighting Aircraft**: Drop water/retardants.
    - **Ambulance Aircraft**: Medical evacuation.
## Based on Range
- **Short-haul Aircraft**: For short distances (e.g., Embraer E175).
- **Medium-haul Aircraft**: For moderate distances (e.g., Boeing 737).
- **Long-haul Aircraft**: For intercontinental travel (e.g., Boeing 787).
## Based on Speed
- **Subsonic Aircraft**: Operate below the speed of sound (e.g., most commercial airliners).
- **Transonic Aircraft**: Operate near the speed of sound (e.g., military aircraft like the F/A-18).
- **Supersonic Aircraft**: Operate faster than the speed of sound (e.g., Concorde, SR-71 Blackbird).
- **Hypersonic Aircraft**: Speeds greater than Mach 5 (e.g., experimental vehicles like X-15).
## Based on Takeoff and Landing
- **Conventional Takeoff and Landing (CTOL)**: Require runways (e.g., Airbus A380).
- **Vertical Takeoff and Landing (VTOL)**: No runway needed (e.g., Harrier Jump Jet, helicopters).
- **Short Takeoff and Landing (STOL)**: Operate on short runways (e.g., De Havilland DHC-6 Twin Otter).
## Based on Size
- **Light Aircraft**: Weigh less than 5,700 kg (e.g., Cessna 172).
- **Heavy Aircraft**: Large commercial or military planes (e.g., Boeing 777).
## Based on Construction Material
- **Metal Aircraft**: Use aluminum, titanium, etc. (e.g., Boeing 737).
- **Composite Aircraft**: Use advanced materials like carbon fiber (e.g., Boeing 787 Dreamliner).
- **Wood Aircraft**: Historical or lightweight aircraft (e.g., De Havilland Mosquito).>)
