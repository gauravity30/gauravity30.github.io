# Critical and Drag Divergence 
*Created on:* 2025-01-20  
*Tags:* #CriticalMach #DragDivergenceMach

## Critical Mach Number

- The free-stream Mach number at which sonic flow is first obtained somewhere on the airfoil surface is called the critical Mach number of the airfoil.
- Characteristic of airfoil.
- Thinner airfoils have higher values of $M_{cr}$.
- The point where locally Mach number is 1 on the airfoil surface has the lowest pressure. This value decreases with increase in thickness and hence the critical Mach number decreases. 
$$
C_{p,cr} = \frac{2}{\gamma M_{\infty}^2} \left\{ \left[ \frac{2+(\gamma-1)M_{\infty}^2}{\gamma+1} \right]^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)} -1 \right\}
$$

$$
c_{p_{min}} = \frac{(c_{p_{0}})_{min}}{\sqrt{ 1-M_{\infty}^2 }}
$$
The point of intersection of the above two curves gives the critical Mach number for an airfoil. The value of $(c_{p_{0}})_{min}$ is obtained from a low-speed, incompressible wind tunnel experiment.

## Drag-Divergence Mach Number

- At Mach numbers greater than $M_{cr}$, pockets of supersonic flows start to emerge corresponding to transonic regime for the airfoil.
- During this period, the drag increases suddenly to a large value.
- Shock waves are dissipative phenomena that increases drag on the airfoil.
- Additionally, they create adverse pressure gradient causing flow to separate from the surface.
- Thus, the sharp increase in $c_{d}$ is a combined effect of shock waves and flow separation. 
- The free-stream Mach number at which $C_{d}$ begins to increase rapidly is defined as the drag-divergence Mach number.
$$
M_{cr} < M_{\text{drag divergence}}< 1
$$
