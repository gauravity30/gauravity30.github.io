## Definition
*The following criteria govern the selection of a propellant:*
- *Molecular Mass:* Lower molecular mass, higher value of $C^*$.
- *High temperature and specific heats:* Lower specific energy causes higher $T_{c}$ for a given value of energy.
- *Specific heat ratio*: Smaller value of $\gamma$ for higher values of $C^*$. Lower influence of $\gamma$ than $T_{c}$ and M.

*1. Influence of Heat Release:*
- Heat release of products ($\Delta H^0_{f,products}$) must be large and negative.
- Heat release of reactants ($\Delta H^0_{f,products}$) must a small value.
  
*2. Influence of Molecular Mass:*
- Fuels with lower M produces lower molecular mass combustion products.
- Fuel rich MR forms products with lower molecular mass => Higher $I_{sp}$.
- Chamber pressure increases the maximum $I_{sp}$.
  
*3. Density:* Higher density propellants will occupy smaller volume and reduces the structural mass.

*4. Stability of fuel and oxidizer:* It shouldn't react simultaneously and explode.

*5. Storability, Corrosiveness and Toxicity:* It should be easy to handle and not harmful to humans and environment.

*6. Cost:* Cheaper propellants increase the commercial viability.
