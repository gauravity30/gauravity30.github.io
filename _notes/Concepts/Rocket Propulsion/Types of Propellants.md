## Definition
*The propellants can be classified into following:*

- *Solid Propellants*
  - [[Double-base]] 
  - [[Composite modified double-base]]
  - [[Composite propellants]]
  - [[Nitramine propellants]]
    
- *Liquid Propellants*
	- Based on Energy content
		-  [[Low Energy LP]]
		- [[Medium Energy LP]]
		- [[High Energy LP]]
	- Based on Storability
		- Launch Vehicles don't require to store fuel.
		- Missiles need prolonged storage.
		- Satellites: Space storable propellants.
		- *Earth Storable:* Almost all low energy propellant
		- *Space Storable:* low FP and high BP is desired. 
		- *Cryogenic/Semi-cryogenic:* They are neither earth nor space storable.
	- Hypergolic and Non-hypergolic propellants
		- *Hypergolic:* Initiation of spontaneous reaction when fuel and oxidizer mixed together.
		- High reactivity of oxidizer => Hypergolic
		- Semi-cryogenic and cryogenic propellant are *non-hypergolic*.
	  
- *Hybrid Propellants*
	- Propellants in different phases.
	- Liquid Oxidizer + Solid Fuel

## Propellant characteristics

- High [[Specific Impulse]]
- Predictable, reproducible and initial adjustable burning rate.
- Minimum variation in thrust.
- High density
- Good aging characteristics 
- Low absorption of moisture
- Low technical risk
- Non-toxic exhaust gases.
- Not prone to combustion instability
