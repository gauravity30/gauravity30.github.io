Planets

Created on: 2024-12-12
Tags: #Planets
Course: Astrobiology

Definition

Two kinds of planets in our solar system

  • Inner - Rocky
  • Outer - Gaseous/Ice Giants

Earth has the highest density among all the planets in solar system.

Heat Sources and Cooling of the Planetary Bodies

Heat Sources:

  • Accretion: The large mass planetary body attracts small bodies flying around it. These collide with the planet’s surface and causes impact heating.
  • Differentiation: Dense materials fall into the core where as light materials rise to the surface. The gravitational potential energy is converted to thermal energy.
  • Radioactive Decay: Mass-energy contained in the nuclei of the planet is converted to thermal energy.

Cooling Mechanisms

  • Eruptions: Volcanic eruptions bring hot lava up from the interior.
  • Radiation: Energy is radiated into the space by the planet.
  • Convection: The hot rock rises and cooler rock falls in a mantle convection cell.
  • Conduction: Heat is carried into the rigid lithosphere through conduction.

Earth’s Interior

  • Core
    • Highest Density
    • NiFe (Nickel and Iron)
    • Inner Core : Solid, 1220 KM thick
    • Outer Core: Liquid, 2250 KM thick
  • Mantle
    • Moderate Density
    • SiMa (Silicon and Magnesium)
    • Semi-solid
    • 2900 KM thick
  • Crust
    • Lowest Density
    • Granite and Basalt
    • SiAl (Silicon and Aluminium)
    • Solid, rigid rock
    • 30-70 KM thick.
    • Continental and Oceanic Crust.

Lithosphere

  • The crust and part of the upper mantle is known as lithosphere.
  • 100 KM thick
  • Less dense than the material below it so it floats.

Asthenosphere

  • The plastic layer below the lithosphere is known as asthenosphere.
  • The plates of the lithosphere float on the asthenosphere.
  • 100 KM to 700 Km below earth’s surface.

Mineral: A natural inorganic substance with a distinct chemical composition and internal structure.

Rock: An aggregate of minerals.

Shaping Planetary Surface

Impact Cratering

  • Impacts by asteriods/meteorites.
  • Ideal sites to understand the deeper regions of a planet otherwise not exposed.
  • Older Region –> More Craters
  • Newer region –> Less Craters.

Volcanism Eruption of molten rock onto surface

Plate Tectonics Disruption of planet’s surface by internal stresses.

Erosion

  • Surface changes made by wind, water or ice.
  • Valleys by glaciers
  • Canyons by rivers.
  • Sand blown by wind.
