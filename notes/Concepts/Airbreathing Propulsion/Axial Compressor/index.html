Axial Compressor

Created on: 2025-02-09
Tags: #AxialCompressor
Course: Airbreathing Propulsion

Basics of turbomachinery

  • The energy transfer between the blades and the fluid takes place in an a turbomachinery through a set of rotating blades called rotors.
  • Stationary blades called the stator follow the rotor blades.
  • A rotor and a stator comprise a turbomachinery stage.
  • The rotors are attached to the hub and hence the reaction of the blade forces and moments is exerted on the fluid.
  • Whereas the stator are cantilevered from the casing and perform no work on the fluid.
  • Absolute observer: An observer sitting on the casing. It sees an unsteady flow through the rotor.
  • Relative observer: An observer sitting on the rotor. It sees a steady flow through the rotor.
  • The components of the velocity are connected through \(\text{Relative Swirl} = \text{Absolute Swirl} - \omega r\)
  • Let the absolute velocity vector and the relative velocity vector be \(\vec{C} = C_{r}\hat{r} + C_{\theta}\hat{\theta} + C_{z}\hat{z}\) \(\vec{W} = W_{r}\hat{r} + W_{\theta}\hat{\theta} + W_{z}\hat{z}\)
  • The rotor blade spins an angular velocity $\omega$, hence it describes a solid body rotation as \(\vec{U} = \omega r\hat{\theta}\)
  • Thus, \(\vec{W} = \vec{C} - \vec{U}\)
  • These vectors form a triangle known as velocity triangle.
  • The $\hat{\theta}$ component of the velocity is referred to as the swirl component.

Euler Turbine Equation

  • The fluid angular momentum about the axis of rotation is given as \(\text{Fluid Angular Momentum} = \dot{m}rC_{\theta}\)
  • The change of fluid angular momentum between the exit and inlet of the stream tube is the applied torque exerted by the blade on the fluid, that is, \(\dot{m}(r_{2}C_{\theta2}-r_{1}C_{\theta1}) = \tau_{fluid}\)
  • The power transmitted to the fluid is given as \(P = \dot{m}\omega \Delta(rC_{\theta})\)

  • The specific work is given as \(w = \frac{P}{\dot{m}}\)

  • The change of total enthalpy of the fluid across the blade row is equal to the specific work \(h_{t2} - h_{t1} = w\)

Axial-flow versus Radial-flow machines

  • In an axial flow turbomachinery, the fluid path is predominantly along the axis of rotation.
  • In radial flow, the fluid path departs from axial and attains a predominantly radial motion at the exit.
  • In axial flow, $r_{1}\approx r_{2}\approx r$.
  • The Euler turbine equation may be simplified to \(w_{c} = U(C_{\theta2} - C_{\theta1})\)
  • Pitchline radius of an axial-flow turbomachinery is assumed to represent the mean or average of the flowfield properties in the annulus and thus serves as the first line of attack in a one-dimensional flow analysis approach. \(r_{m} = \frac{r_{h}+r_{t}}{2}\)

Axial-flow compressors

  • Provide mechanical compression for the airstream that enters a gas turbine engine.
  • For large pressure rise, stages are stacked together.
  • In a stage, the rotor blade imparts angular momentum to the fluid, while the stator blade row removes the angular momentum from the fluid.

  • The rotor and stator torques are given as \(\tau_{rotor} = -\dot{m}\cdot r(C_{\theta2}-C_{\theta1})\) \(\tau_{stator} = -\dot{m}\cdot r(C_{\theta3}-C_{\theta2})\)
  • We assume that the absolute swirl and angular momentum across the stage remains the same i.e. $C_{\theta1}=C_{\theta3}$ \(\tau_{rotor}=-\tau_{stator}\)

  • The flow angle are measured with respect to the axial direction and labeled as $\alpha$ and $\beta$ which correspond to the absolute and the relative flow velocity vector $\vec{C}$ and $\vec{W}$.

  • The relative velocity vector at the exit of the rotor $\vec{W}{2}$ should nearly be tangent to the rotor suction surface at the trailing edge. Hence, $\beta{2}$ is fixed by the geometry of the rotor and remains nearly constant over a wide operating range of the compressor.
  • Similarly $\alpha_{1}$ or $\alpha_{3}$ stay constant over the operating range.
  • The flow angles $\alpha_{2}$ and $\beta_{1}$ change with rotor speed $U$.
  • A repeated stage or normal stage refers to \(\vec{C}_{3} = \vec{C}_{1}\) \(\alpha_{3} = \alpha_{1}\)
  • As a result, $ \beta_{2} = \alpha_{1} $ and $ \alpha_{2} = \beta_{1} $.
  • The flow coefficient is defined as \(\phi = \frac{C_{z}}{U}\)
  • The stage degree of reaction $^\circ R$ is defined as the fraction of static enthalpy rise across the stage that is accomplished by the rotor.

  • The stator takes out the swirl put in by the rotor and thus converts it to a static pressure rise. \(^\circ R = \frac{h_{2}-h_{1}}{h_{3}-h_{2}} = 1 - \frac{C_{\theta1} + C_{\theta2}}{2U}\)

  • The flow is more stable over the rotor as compared to the stator, hence a slightly higher burden of static pressure rise is allocated to the rotor.
