Turbojet Engine

Created on: 2025-02-08
Tags: #Turbojet Engine
Course: Airbreathing Propulsion

Definition

  • It is gas generator fitted with an inlet and exhaust system.
  • The performance of a turbojet depends on the inlet and exhaust component efficiencies.

Inlet

  • The basic function of the inlet is to deliver the air to the compressor at the right Mach number $M_{2}$ and the right quality (low distortion).
  • The subsonic compressors are designed for an axial Mach number of $M_{2}=0.5-0.6$.
  • The inlet needs to decelerate the air for the compressor in case the flight Mach number is higher than 0.5 and 0.6.
  • It is also known as a diffuser and associated with adverse pressure gradient which leads to flow separation.
  • Designing a inlet without flow separation is the major objective.
  • An ideal inlet provides isentropic compression of the captured flow.
  • The entropy rise in an adiabatic process leads to a total pressure loss $\Delta p_{t}$ from the Gibbs equation.
  • The inlet adiabatic efficiency is defined as \(\eta_{d} = \frac{h_{t2s} - h_{0}}{h_{t_{2}}-h_{0}} = \frac{(V^2/2)_{ideal}}{V_{0}^2/2}\)
  • The state $s$ represents the ideal state which would have been obtained through isentropic compression but in real-life adiabatic but not reversible due to wall friction.
  • The efficiency represents the amount of energy lost as heat due to friction.
  • The pressure is related as \(\frac{p_{t_{2}}}{p_{0}} = \left(1+\frac{\eta_{d}(\gamma-1)}{2}M_{0}^2\right)^{\gamma/\gamma-1}\)
  • The inlet total pressure recovery factor is given as \(\pi_{d} = \frac{p_{t2}}{p_{t0}} = 1\, (\text{For isentropic compression})\)

Compressor

  • The power delivered by the rotor spinning at angular speed $\omega$ is given as \(P = \tau \cdot \omega\)
  • The compressor is driven by the power extracted from the turbine.
  • The flow is adiabatic since the energy transferred through the walls is extremely larger than the amount delivered by it.

  • The pressure and temperature increase in the compressor is as given as \(\pi_{c} = \frac{p_{t3}}{p_{t2}}, \,\,\, \tau_{c} = \frac{T_{t3}}{T_{t2}}\)

  • The measure of irreversibility in a compressor is defined using
    • Adiabatic efficiency $\eta_{c}$
    • Polytropic efficiency $e_{e}$

  • The adiabatic efficiency is defined as \(\eta_{c} = \frac{P_{ideal}}{P_{actual}} = \frac{h_{t3s}-h_{t2}}{h_{t3}-h_{t2}} = \frac{\pi_{c}^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma} -1}{\tau_{c}-1}\)
  • Polytropic efficiency is defined as \(e_{c} = \frac{dh_{ts}}{dh_{t}}\)
  • It is also known as small stage efficiency and the adiabatic efficiency of a compressor with small pressure ratio or an individual stage. \(\tau_{c} = \pi_{c}^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma e_{c}}\) \(\eta_{c} = \frac{\pi_{c}^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}-1}{\pi_{c}^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma e_{c}}-1}\)
  • The adiabatic efficiency i.e. efficiency of the whole compressor is dependent on the efficiency of each single stage or the polytropic efficiency.
  • $e_{c}$ is usually maintained constant in an engine.

Combustion Chamber

  • The air is mixed with the fuel and a chemical reaction ensues which is exothermic.
  • The ideal burner operates at same total pressure i.e. $P_{t3} = P_{t4}$.
  • Whereas in a real burner, the pressure drops due to irreversibility and \(\pi_{b} <1\)
  • The energy balance applied to the combustion chamber gives \(\dot{m}_{0}h_{t3} + \dot{m_{f}}Q_{R}\eta_{b} = (\dot{m_{0}} + \dot{m}_{f})h_{t4}\)

    Turbine

  • It is involved with significant pressure drop called flow expansion.
  • The flow expansion produces the necessary power for the compressor and the propulsive power for the aircraft.

  • The first few stages (HPT) of the turbine are cooled due to the high temperature flow exiting the combustor.

  • The adiabatic efficiency across the turbine is defined as \(\eta_{t} = \frac{h_{t4}-h_{t5}}{h_{t4}-h_{t5s}} = \frac{\Delta h_{t,actual}}{\Delta h_{t,isentropic}} = \frac{1-\tau_{t}}{1-\pi_{t}^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}}\)
  • The small stage efficiency for the turbine is defined as \(e_{t} = \frac{dh_{t}}{dh_{ts}}\)
  • It is related to the adiabatic efficiency as \(\eta_{t} = \frac{1-\tau_{t}}{1-\tau_{t}^{1/e_{t}}}\)
  • Ideally, the compressor absorbs all the turbine shaft power i.e. \(P_{t} = P_{c} \implies (\dot{m_{0}} + \dot{m}_{f})(h_{t4}-h_{t_{5}}) = \dot{m}_{0}(h_{t3}-h_{t2})\)
  • The mechanical efficiency of the power transfer is given as \(P_{c} = \eta_{m}P_{t}\)

Nozzle

  • The function of a nozzle is to accelerate the gas efficiently.
  • The gross thrust is given as \(F_{g} = \dot{m}_{9}V_{9} + (p_{9}-p_{0})A_{9}\)
  • The real flows are considered as adiabatic.
  • The maximum gross thrust is obtained for a perfectly expanded nozzle.
  • The exit pressure is a direct function of the nozzle pressure ratio (NPR). \(NPR = \frac{P_{t7}}{P_{0}}\)
  • For $NPR>NPR_{critical}$, the flow through nozzle is choked i.e. the throat velocity is sonic.
  • $NPR_{critical} \approx 2$.

Efficiency of the engine

  • The thermal efficiency of a turbojet is given as \(\eta_{th} = 1- \frac{T_{0}}{T_{t3}}\)
  • The propulsive efficiency of the turbojet is given as \(\eta_{p} = \frac{2}{1+\left( \frac{V_{9}}{V_{0}} \right)}\)

    Afterburner

  • Helps in increase of thrust of an aircraft gas turbine engine.

  • The pressure ratio across the afterburner is given as \(\pi_{AB} = \frac{P_{t7}}{P_t5}\)
  • The energy balance across the afterburner is given as \((\dot{m}_{0}+\dot{m}_{f}+\dot{m}_{f,AB})h_{t7} - (\dot{m}_{0}+\dot{m}_{f})h_{t5} = \dot{m}_{f,AB}Q_{R,AB}\eta_{AB}\)
