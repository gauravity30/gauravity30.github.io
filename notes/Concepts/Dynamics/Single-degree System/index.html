Single-degree System

Created on: 2025-02-01
Tags: #SingleDegreeSystem
Course: Dynamics

Definition

  • A system with single degree of freedom.
  • The simplest system is a spring-mass system.

Free Vibration

  • No external force is applied on the mass.
  • The motion results from the initial disturbance is known as free-vibration.
  • It can be damped/undamped.

Undamped Free Vibration

  • A spring-mass system is initially disturbed and the motion is observed.
  • The mass of the body is $m$.
  • The spring constant of the spring is $k$.
  • The motion can be written as \(m\ddot{x} + kx = 0\)
  • This is a homogenous second-order ODE where $a=1, b=0$ and $c=\frac{k}{m}$. The value of $D = -\frac{4k}{m}$.
  • Thus, the solution of the equation is in the form \(x(t) = A_{1}\cos(\omega_{n}t) +A_{2}\sin(\omega_{n}t)\)
  • $\omega_{n}$ is the natural frequency of the system and equal to the root of the ratio of k and m. \(\omega_{n} = \sqrt{ \frac{k}{m} }\)
  • Putting the boundary conditions, we get \(x(t) = x_{0}\cos(\omega_{n}t) + \frac{\dot{x_{0}}}{\omega_{n}}\sin (\omega_{n}t)\)
  • This is a harmonic motion and known as a harmonic oscillator.

Damped Free Vibration

  • The viscous damping force $F$ is proportional to the velocity $\dot{x}$.
  • The equation of motion can be written as \(m\ddot{x} + c\dot{x}+kx = 0\)
  • This is a homogenous second-order ODE.
  • The general solution of ODE is \(x(t) = C_{1}e^{s_{1}t} + C_{2}e^{s_{2}t}\)
  • Where \(s_{1,2} = \frac{-c \pm \sqrt{ c^2 - 4mk}}{2m}\)
  • The system is critically damped when \(\left( \frac{c_{c}}{2m} \right)^2 - \frac{k}{m} = 0\)
  • The damping ratio is the ratio between damping factor and critical damping factor \(\zeta = \frac{c}{c_{c}} = \frac{c}{2\omega_{n}m} = \frac{c}{2\sqrt{ km }}\)

  • The value of $s_{1,2}$ can be written as \(s_{1,2} = (-\zeta \pm \sqrt{ \zeta^2 -1 })\omega_{n}\)

  • The system is underdamped if $\zeta<0$, the system is critically damped for $\zeta=1$ and overdamped for $\zeta>1$.

  • The frequency of oscillation of the damped system is given as \(\omega_{d} = \sqrt{1-\zeta^2 }\,\omega_{n}\)

  • A critically damped system will return to its equilibrium position at earliest.

Forced Vibrations

  • A damped spring-mass system is driven with a force $F(t)$.
  • The equation of motion is a non-homogenous second-order ODE \(m\ddot{x} + c\dot{x} + kx = F(t)\)

Undamped System under Harmonic Force

  • The governing ODE is \(m\ddot{x} + kx = F_{0}\cos(\omega t)\)
  • The homogenous solution $x_{h}(t)$ is already known \(x_{h}(t) = C_{1}\cos(\omega_{n} t) + C_{2}\sin(\omega_{n} t)\)
  • The exciting force is harmonic, hence the particular solution is assumed to a harmonic function oscillating with the same frequency \(x_{p}(t) = X\cos(\omega t)\)
  • Substitution of $x_{p}(t)$ in to the ODE, we get \(X = \frac{F_{0}}{k - m\omega^2} = \frac{\delta_{st}}{1-\left( \frac{\omega}{\omega_{n}} \right)^2}\)
  • $\delta_{st}$ is the static deflection of the mass under a force $F_{0}$.
  • The total solution is \(x(t) = C_{1}\cos(\omega_{n}t) + C_{2}\sin(\omega_{n}t) + \frac{F_{0}}{k-m\omega^2}\,\cos(\omega t)\)

  • Putting the initial conditions, we get \(C_{1} = x_{0} - \frac{F_{0}}{k-m\omega^2},\,\,\, C_{2} = \frac{\dot{x_{0}}}{\omega_{n}}\)

  • The quantity $\frac{X}{\delta_{st}}$ is known as amplification/magnification factor
  • Case 1: $0<\frac{\omega}{\omega_{n}}<1$, the response is in phase with the external force.
  • Case 2: $\frac{\omega}{\omega_{n}}>1$, the response is out of phase with the external force.

  • Case 3: $\frac{\omega}{\omega_{n}}=1$, the amplitude $X$ becomes infinite and the response blows up. This is known as resonance.

  • If the forcing frequency $\omega$ is close to the natural frequency $\omega_{n}$ but not equal, a phenomenon known as beating occurs.
  • The amplitude builds up and then diminishes in a regular pattern.

Damped System under Harmonic Force

  • The governing ODE is given as \(m\ddot{x} + c\dot{x} + kx = F_{0}\cos(\omega t)\)
  • The particular solution is also expected to be harmonic \(x_{p}(t) = X\cos(\omega t - \phi)\)
  • $X$ is the amplitude and $\phi$ is the phase angle.
  • Substitution of particular solution and equating the coefficients, we get \(X = \frac{F_{0}}{\sqrt{(k-m\omega^2)^2 + c^2\omega^2}}\) \(\phi = \tan^{-1} \left( \frac{c\omega}{k-m\omega^2} \right)\)

  • The magnification factor is given as \(\frac{X}{\delta_{st}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{ (1-r^2)^2 + (2\zeta r)^2}}\) Where $r=\frac{\omega}{\omega_{n}}$

  • Any amount of damping reduces the magnification factor (M) for all the values of forcing frequency.
